## 1. Irwin (2021)
- **Authors:** Douglas A. Irwin
- **Year:** 2021
- **Title:** The rise and fall of import substitution
- **Journal:** World Development, vol. 139, article 105306
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.worlddev.2020.105306
- **Summary:** The paper traces how import substitution, meaning restricting manufactured imports to promote industrialization, became the favoured trade strategy of the 1950s and was widely abandoned by the mid-1960s, even by its supporters. It finds that early advocates were cautious in their support and were among the first to question the policy, based on evidence from country experiences.

## 6. Brandt & Thun (2010)
- **Authors:** Loren Brandt; Eric Thun
- **Year:** 2010
- **Title:** The Fight for the Middle: Upgrading, Competition, and Industrial Development in China
- **Journal:** World Development, vol. 38, no. 11, pp. 1555-1574
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.worlddev.2010.05.003
- **Summary:** When China joined the WTO, many feared that domestic firms would lose market share to foreign-invested enterprises (FIEs). Instead, aggregate data and three sector studies show indigenous firms gradually gaining share and deepening their capabilities. The authors argue that China is a new development model: upgrading is driven by the domestic market, and intense competition between domestic and foreign firms there is what drives the upgrading of domestic firms.

## 7. Brandt & Thun (2016)
- **Authors:** Loren Brandt; Eric Thun
- **Year:** 2016
- **Title:** Constructing a Ladder for Growth: Policy, Markets, and Industrial Upgrading in China
- **Journal:** World Development, vol. 80, pp. 78-95
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.worlddev.2015.11.001
- **Summary:** In large domestic markets, the low-end segment naturally shields local firms from foreign competition, while higher-end segments draw foreign firms to localize and so open channels for capability building. Three Chinese industrial sectors show that restrictive demand- and supply-side policies, though meant to help local firms, often cancelled out these advantages and "knocked out rungs" of the upgrading ladder.

## 12. Ciani & Stiebale (2024)
- **Authors:** Andrea Ciani; Joel Stiebale
- **Year:** 2024
- **Title:** Export Performance Under Domestic Anti-Dumping Protection
- **Journal:** World Development, vol. 183, article 106716
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.worlddev.2024.106716
- **Summary:** Using Peruvian firm-level data and product-specific anti-dumping (AD) measures, the paper finds that how import protection affects export performance depends on which country the AD duties target. Duties on China go with higher export prices and smaller shipments, mainly among small firms, while duties on middle- or high-income competitors go with larger export quantities and lower unit values, which fits with scale economies.

## 15. Lee, Lee & Lee (2021)
- **Authors:** Keun Lee; Jongho Lee; Juneyoung Lee
- **Year:** 2021
- **Title:** Variety of national innovation systems (NIS) and alternative pathways to growth beyond the middle-income stage: Balanced, imbalanced, catching-up, and trapped NIS
- **Journal:** World Development, vol. 144, article 105472
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.worlddev.2021.105472
- **Summary:** Using US patent data for 32-35 economies, the authors measure national innovation systems on five variables and identify two catching-up pathways beyond middle income. One is a "balanced" NIS (Ireland, Spain, Hong Kong, Singapore, and recently India and Russia). The other is an "imbalanced" NIS (Korea, Taiwan, now China) with short technology cycle times, low originality, high localization and high diversification. These contrast with a "trapped" NIS (e.g., Brazil, Argentina, Mexico, South Africa, Malaysia, Thailand) that shows the opposite attributes.

## 16. Gerring, Gjerløw & Knutsen (2022)
- **Authors:** John Gerring; Haakon Gjerløw; Carl Henrik Knutsen
- **Year:** 2022
- **Title:** Regimes and industrialization
- **Journal:** World Development, vol. 152, article 105791
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.worlddev.2021.105791
- **Summary:** The authors argue that autocracies are more likely than democracies to pursue heavy-industry-centred development because of different social bases, security concerns and policy tools, which give them stronger incentives and capabilities for rapid industrialization. Tests on a 200-year, near-global dataset suggest that industrialization is one of the few areas where autocracies may have a significant advantage over democracies.

## 22. Gallagher, Moreno-Brid & Porzecanski (2008)
- **Authors:** Kevin P. Gallagher; Juan Carlos Moreno-Brid; Roberto Porzecanski
- **Year:** 2008
- **Title:** The Dynamism of Mexican Exports: Lost in (Chinese) Translation?
- **Journal:** World Development, vol. 36, no. 8, pp. 1365-1380
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.worlddev.2007.08.004
- **Summary:** Mexico's main non-oil exports are losing dynamism in the US market: their share is falling, or growing more slowly than China's, a trend that began after China joined the WTO. Possible causes include real appreciation of the peso against an undervalued yuan, falling public infrastructure investment, limited bank credit, and the lack of Mexican policy to spur innovation and strengthen domestic backward and forward linkages.

## 23. Fu, Pietrobelli & Soete (2011)
- **Authors:** Xiaolan Fu; Carlo Pietrobelli; Luc Soete
- **Year:** 2011
- **Title:** The Role of Foreign Technology and Indigenous Innovation in the Emerging Economies: Technological Change and Catching-up
- **Journal:** World Development, vol. 39, no. 7, pp. 1204-1212
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.worlddev.2010.05.009
- **Summary:** The article argues that, despite globalization and liberal trade, emerging economies gain from international technology diffusion only when they make parallel indigenous innovation efforts and have modern institutions, governance and supportive innovation systems. Because Northern technology is often inappropriate for the South, indigenous and foreign innovation efforts are complementary rather than substitutes.
