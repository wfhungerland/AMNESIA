# Displacement Before Escalation: outline (updated 22.11.2026 to the full draft)

Working title: **Displacement Before Escalation: Late Industrialisation and the Timing of Incumbent Constraint, 1850–2024**

Target: *World Development*. Length target 8,000 words including references (third-party figure, to be checked against the official Guide for Authors). Figures: 3 to 4. Tables: 3 in the text, the rest in appendices.

Each section lists its key sentences. Every number has a source in `data/` or in a cited work; the check table `manuscript/claims_check.csv` is kept from the first draft onwards.

## 1. Introduction (≈ 900 words)

- Late industrialisers face external constraint from incumbent powers: tariffs, quotas, export restraints, technology denial. Development economics treats this constraint as background.
- Question: when does incumbent constraint arrive in the catch-up process: during technology absorption, or after the challenger has displaced the incumbent in contested markets?
- Answer: the reach of constraint matches the reach of displacement. Of 41 coded responses, 36 follow their sector's milestone; only one broad instrument (IR 4–5) precedes it, the export controls of October 2022. Checks against selection on the outcome: a complete register of 23 broad US instruments (22 of 31 pairs after the export crossover; textile regime the second exception); a panel of 1,316 manufacturing groups (broad instruments 5.7 times as frequent after displacement, Japan and Korea); population hazard (about one displaced group in five draws a broad instrument within ten years). Product remedies (AD/CVD) track US imports from the challenger, not world-market displacement.
- Two refinements: (a) escalation tracks displacement in sectors with political voice in the incumbent (British sugar and steel, not dyes); (b) growth alone does not trigger escalation (the United States overtook Britain in income around 1900 without displacing it in third markets, and drew no comparable reaction). In the US population, neither import volume nor employment (NBER-CES) predicts which displaced sectors draw broad instruments.
- Contribution: a dated, sector-level sequencing test across 175 years that endogenises the incumbent, which the developmental-state, convergence and trade-politics literatures treat as exogenous or static.
- Claim discipline: sequencing, not causation.

## 2. Literature and gap (≈ 1,100 words)

Source: `research/projekt/... Literature Positioning Across Five Fields.md`.

- Developmental state (Gerschenkron, Johnson, Amsden, Wade, Evans, Chang): the incumbent is held still; Chang identifies the ladder pattern but gives it no clock.
- Convergence (Eichengreen, Park and Shin; Acemoglu, Aghion and Zilibotti): deceleration is explained from within; external constraint untested.
- Political economy of protection (Grossman and Helpman; Bown and Crowley): who and how much, not when; Bown and Crowley test lagged import growth at product level, without an intermediate stage or a long horizon.
- Technology denial (Mastanduno; Lind and Mastanduno): timing indexed to threat perception, not displacement.
- IP history (Khan; Moser): weak-IP catch-up is documented, the timing of incumbent complaint is not.
- One paragraph on what the paper adds over Bown and Crowley.

## 3. Design (≈ 1,300 words)

- Cases and incumbents; why these four (largest late industrialisers measured against a frontier incumbent).
- **Displacement milestone:** first year the challenger leads in the market where the incumbent sells, by sector (18 milestones, Table 1). Robustness: uniform export rule (UN Comtrade; Tyszynski 1951 for Germany).
- **Incumbent responses:** 41 events, each coded on the instrument-reach scale 1–5 (rubric in Appendix E), two coders, Krippendorff's α reported.
- **Phase and lag:** absorption = before the sector's milestone; lag = response year minus milestone year. Economy-wide measures are dated against the case's first milestone.
- **Independent attention measure:** Historic Hansard 1850–1914, 15,126 economic debates; mentions of Germany and of the United States per 100,000 words; sector split.
- **Falsification condition**, stated ex ante: the claim fails if binding instruments (IR ≥ 4) systematically precede displacement, or if the lags are fully explained by administrative processing times.

## 4. Results: the sequence in four cases (≈ 2,000 words)

- **Germany and Britain.** Chemicals export lead by 1899 (1.43 times British exports), with almost no parliamentary voice; sugar (1880) the first sector where Germany becomes a political issue, ending in the Brussels Convention of 1902 (IR 4); steel and machinery displaced in export markets only between 1899 and 1913 (0.35 to 0.91; 0.50 to 1.33), when attention to Germany in economic debates roughly triples and Tariff Reform starts (1903). Comprehensive protection never comes before 1932.
- **Japan and the United States.** Textiles (1951 → restraint 1957), television (1970 → antidumping 1971, OMA 1977), steel (1969 → VRA 1969, trigger prices 1978), cars (1980 → VER 1981), semiconductors (1981/1987 → agreement 1986, tariffs 1987). All binding instruments after their sector milestone; lags 0 to 9 years.
- **Korea.** Section 301 IP case (1985) and IP agreement (1986) precede DRAM leadership but score 2 and 3; antidumping 1993 one year after leadership. Shipbuilding: 18-year lag to Japan's WTO complaints, consistent with the weak political voice of the declining Japanese industry.
- **China.** Binding responses follow manufacturing (2010), solar and telecoms (2012) by zero to eight years. The October 2022 export controls are the bounded exception: preventive denial in one sector before any market lead.
- **Complete register and population (Section 4.7).** 23 broad US instruments, 31 instrument–target pairs: 22 after the export crossover, 4 before (2 artefacts: semiconductor group too coarse, global steel safeguard; 2 substantive: textile LTA 1962 and agreements 1971 against Korea), 5 not datable. Population: broad instrument within 10 years after displacement in 22 % (Japan), 20 % (Korea), 1 % (China without the 2018 Section 301 tariffs).
- **Control group and weight.** Panel of 1,316 group series: start of targeted broad instruments 0.24 → 1.15 per 100 group-years (IRR 5.7, Japan and Korea); Section 301 tariffs covered all 813 Chinese groups. AD/CVD cases track US imports from the challenger (1 / 11 / 30 % by third), not displacement once export size is held constant (IRR 1.10). Employment (NBER-CES) adds nothing beyond imports and does not predict broad instruments.
- Table 2: responses by case and phase. Figure 1: timeline of milestones and responses by case (lags).

## 5. Political voice and the American non-case (≈ 900 words)

- Sector split of the Hansard record (Table E4): sugar 2.7 → 17.2 per cent of sector debates linking Germany; steel 15 → 24 per cent; dyes almost never debated.
- The United States as a placebo: income overtaking around 1900, but in 17 fixed third markets US exports reach only 0.51 of British exports outside Canada by 1913 (Germany 0.85); in manufactures 0.42 against Germany's 0.88 (Tyszynski 1951); no rise in parliamentary attention.
- Interpretation: the trigger is displacement in contested export markets in sectors with voice. In the US population, neither import volume nor employment explains which displaced sectors draw broad instruments; what creates voice stays open.

## 6. Convergence after displacement (≈ 700 words)

- Total factor productivity relative to the United States (PWT 11.0, ctfp): Japan peaks at 0.80 in 1991 and falls to 0.64 by 2023; Korea peaks at 0.71 in 2002 and stays between 0.62 and 0.70; Germany at or above 1.0 since 2000; China at 0.47 in 2023 and still rising, with a pause between 2007 and 2014.
- Descriptive claim only: the two completed East Asian cases stall at 0.70 to 0.80 of the frontier, after the escalation phase. Germany is the exception. Regression to the mean (Pritchett and Summers 2014) is observationally equivalent and acknowledged.
- One paragraph in the outlook on basic research, without a test.

## 7. Limits and conclusion (≈ 600 words)

- Small N; non-independence of cases treated as a feature; selection on successful catch-ups (negative controls Thailand and Vietnam as future work).
- Measurement: population check by world exports understates home-market displacement; AD cases matched to SITC through HS 1988/92 concordances; employment allocated by tariff lines. Title-filtered debates; parliamentary check only for Britain; several milestones rest on one or two secondary sources (listed in Appendix E).
- Policy reading for late industrialisers: constraint arrives when they win contested markets in politically weighty sectors; the 2022 controls show the window can close earlier.
