# Displacement Before Escalation: Positioning a Sequencing Claim Across Five Literatures

*This paper argues that no existing literature tests the temporal order between measurable technological displacement and incumbent escalation, because each of the five relevant fields treats one side of that relation as exogenous, which is precisely the gap a sequencing study of Germany, Japan, South Korea and China can fill.*

## Key Takeaways 1. The developmental state canon models catch-up as institutional substitution but leaves the incumbent analytically static, a limitation inherited from Gerschenkron (1962), where the leader defines the productivity gap without ever reacting to its closure; Chang (2002) identifies the structural pattern of incumbent restriction without specifying when it begins.
2. The convergence literature quantifies where growth stalls, with roughly 85 % of the output growth slowdown attributable to TFP deceleration (Eichengreen, Park and Shin 2012), yet attributes deceleration exclusively to endogenous causes such as misallocation, with potential manufacturing TFP gains of 30-50 % in China from reallocation alone (Hsieh and Klenow 2009: 1403), leaving exogenous incumbent intervention untested as a reinforcing mechanism.
3. The nearest existing sequencing test is product-bilateral rather than systemic: a one standard deviation rise in recent bilateral import growth raises the probability of an antidumping tariff by 35 % (Bown and Crowley 2013a: 1072), a finding framed within terms-of-trade theory and containing no intermediate rhetorical stage between displacement and binding policy.

## Introduction
The claim under examination is narrow and temporal: in four catch-up episodes spanning Germany from 1840, Japan from 1950, South Korea from 1960 and China from 1978, measurable technological displacement precedes rhetorical escalation, and rhetorical escalation precedes binding trade policy intervention by the incumbent power. The claim asserts neither a general law nor policy effectiveness. Its contribution is sequencing.

Positioning such a claim is unusually demanding because it sits at the intersection of five literatures that between them cover nearly all of its empirical territory while leaving its central relation untested. Each field has an account of one half of the displacement-escalation dyad and treats the other half as given. The developmental state tradition explains how challengers acquire technology and holds the incumbent constant. The convergence literature explains why growth decelerates near the frontier and holds external policy constant. The political economy of protection explains which industries obtain protection and holds time constant. The technology denial literature explains why controls tighten and indexes that timing to strategic perception rather than to displacement metrics. The economic history of intellectual property establishes that catch-up economies used weak IP regimes and holds the timing of incumbent complaint unexamined. What follows maps the argument onto each field in turn, identifying confirmation, extension, contradiction, gap and overclaiming risk.

## The developmental state canon holds the incumbent analytically still
The catch-up literature is a supply-side literature, and its blind spot is structural rather than accidental. Gerschenkron (1962) defines backwardness as distance from a leader whose own behaviour never enters the model; Britain supplies the benchmark against which German universal banking or Russian state direction appear as institutional substitutes, but Britain never responds. Johnson (1982) traces MITI's administrative guidance to 1975, terminating before the 1981 automobile voluntary export restraint, the 1986 semiconductor agreement, and Japan's 1989 designation under Super 301, which is to say before the escalation phase that a sequencing study most needs. Amsden (1989) and Wade (1990) treat United States pressure as an element of the environment Korean and Taiwanese planners navigated rather than as a variable to be explained. Evans (1995) records Section 301 pressure on Brazil's informatics reservation as a political fact. Lall (1992) grades firm capabilities from operational through adaptive to innovative without asking at which grade incumbents begin withholding technology.

Chang (2002) approaches the argument most closely and stops just short of it. He establishes that today's leaders deployed the same protective and imitative instruments during their own ascent and later demanded liberalisation from others, invoking List's 1841 image of the discarded ladder. This is the structural regularity. What it lacks is a clock: no threshold at which restriction begins, no trigger converting tolerance into hostility, no cross-case test of whether the transition recurs at a comparable point. The present claim supplies the temporal specification that the entire Gerschenkron-descended tradition brackets out. The corresponding overclaiming risk is to assert that this literature ignores incumbent reaction; the defensible claim is that it treats reaction as exogenous context rather than as an endogenous variable indexed to displacement.

## Convergence research decomposes the ceiling without asking who else is pushing on it
Growth deceleration near the frontier is among the better-quantified regularities in development economics, and it is explained almost entirely from within. Eichengreen, Park and Shin (2012) locate slowdowns bimodally at approximately $10,000-11,000 and $15,000-16,000 in 2005 purchasing power parity terms and attribute roughly 85 % of the output growth slowdown to TFP deceleration; their later treatment confirms the bimodal structure on an expanded sample (Eichengreen, Park and Shin 2014). Felipe, Abdon and Kumar (2012) express the same phenomenon as a threshold of sustained growth, 4.7 % annually to exit the lower-middle-income trap and 3.5 % to exit the upper-middle-income trap. Acemoglu, Aghion and Zilibotti (2006) supply the theoretical mechanism, a required switch from investment-based to innovation-based strategy as distance to frontier closes, with stagnation where domestic political economy blocks the switch.

The proximate causes canvassed in this literature are uniformly domestic: misallocation, with counterfactual manufacturing TFP gains of 30-50 % in China from equalising marginal products (Hsieh and Klenow 2009), factor accumulation exhausting itself (Young 1995), extractive institutions suppressing creative destruction (Acemoglu and Robinson 2012), talent misallocation (Agénor and Canuto 2012), and a shrinking industrial escalator (Rodrik 2016). Nothing in this canon distinguishes deceleration caused by running out of cheap imitation from deceleration caused by having imitation blocked. The extension available here is to treat the two as jointly operative near the frontier, with incumbent intervention reinforcing a ceiling the endogenous mechanisms would generate more slowly. The World Bank (2024) gestures at the connection by noting that geopolitical tension can slow knowledge diffusion to middle-income economies, without formalising or timing it. The overclaiming risk is precise: the 60-75 % of frontier productivity band is an empirical regularity observable in the four cases, not a consensus threshold this literature has established, since the Eichengreen income thresholds sit well below it.

## Protection models are static where the claim is temporal
The political economy of trade protection answers who and how much with considerable rigour and answers when hardly at all. Grossman and Helpman (1994) model protection as the equilibrium of a menu auction between lobbies and a government weighing contributions against welfare, a formulation with no time index and therefore no place for displacement as a trigger. Rogowski (1989) predicts protectionist coalitions from factor endowments without distinguishing a lag of months from a lag of decades. Milner (1988) explains why some injured sectors demand protection and others do not, mediated by firm internationalisation, through a two-period comparison rather than a continuous trace. Irwin (2017) documents the historical coincidence of displacement and protection across two centuries of United States policy as narrative rather than as a sequencing test. Destler (1992) records the 12-18 month institutional lag from petition to duty as a feature of trade law, which matters here because that interval is partly administrative and must not be read as strategic timing.

Two works come materially closer than the rest. Bhagwati and co-authors (1987) model quid pro quo investment in an explicitly two-period structure where period-one export growth determines period-two protection probability, and Blonigen and Feenstra (1997) confirm empirically that anticipated protection shaped Japanese direct investment, which validates the temporal regularity from the challenger's vantage point. Bown and Crowley (2013a) provide the only direct test of the sequence itself, with the 35 % effect of lagged bilateral import growth on antidumping probability, complemented by evidence that unemployment and real appreciation predict new barriers (Bown and Crowley 2013b). Rodrik (1994) had already flagged the field's inability to explain the dynamics and timing of protection as an open problem. The contribution available here is to move the test from the product-bilateral to the systemic level, to insert rhetorical escalation as an observable intermediate stage, and to extend the window across 175 years; the overclaiming risk is to present as novel what Bown and Crowley demonstrate at finer resolution.

## Technology denial has a timing literature indexed to threat, not to displacement
Cold War technology control offers the closest institutional analogue to contemporary export restriction and the sharpest contrast in scope. Mastanduno (1992) distinguishes a narrow strategic embargo aimed at items of direct military significance, favoured by European members, from a broader economic warfare posture pursued intermittently by Washington; even the broad variant restricted finished goods and specific technologies rather than the upstream toolchain producing them. The regime's practical limits are visible in the 1982 dispute over the Urengoy-Pomary-Uzhhorod pipeline, where extraterritorial sanctions on turbine and pipe-laying equipment met open European defiance, were lifted in November 1982 after five months, and did not prevent completion of the 4,500 km line in 1984. The Toshiba-Kongsberg case revealed in 1987 concerned propeller-milling machines with an identifiable military end use, and the CHINCOM China Differential of 1952-1957 applied stricter controls than CoCom to an economy with negligible absorptive capacity.

Against that baseline the October 2022 restrictions represent a change in kind. They act simultaneously on advanced computing chips, fabrication equipment, electronic design automation software, high-bandwidth memory and the participation of United States persons, targeting chokepoints across an entire civilian manufacturing stack. Lind and Mastanduno (2025) compare the two regimes directly and identify the operative asymmetry: unlike the Soviet Union, China absorbs and innovates upon acquired technology, which pushes policy from maintaining a relative lead toward absolute denial. What no work in this field supplies is a timing model indexed to displacement. Mastanduno times controls to confrontation cycles; Drezner (1999) times statecraft to conflict expectations. The overclaiming risk is the word unprecedented, since CoCom did control some fabrication equipment from the mid-1980s; the defensible claim concerns the breadth and civilian orientation of simultaneous chokepoint targeting, and it should acknowledge that the security-versus-competition distinction is itself contested.

## Intellectual property history documents the pattern and omits its clock
That catch-up economies operated under weak intellectual property regimes is close to settled. Khan (2004) shows that United States federal statute explicitly condoned piracy of foreign works from 1790 to 1891 and concludes that copyright regime choice was endogenous to development level. Moser (2005) finds effective innovation in countries without patent laws, and Moser and Voena (2012) exploit the seizure of German-owned patents under the 1917 Trading with the Enemy Act to show that removing protection raised domestic innovation in affected sectors, quasi-experimental evidence that weak protection during catch-up is functional rather than merely tolerated. Lerner (2002) documents the cross-country correlation between wealth and patent strength while conceding that causality runs unresolved. Boldrin and Levine (2008) argue that incumbent industries seek protection precisely as their advantage erodes, which is structurally the present claim stated without a time series.

The historical record contains one long-observed lag and one suggestive modern alignment. British authors petitioned Congress from 1837, Dickens lobbied in 1842 and again in 1867-68, and international copyright arrived only in 1891, a 55-year interval that Khan explains through domestic political economy, namely the point at which American publishers and authors became net rights holders. In the modern period, the 1984 Trade and Tariff Act created intellectual property grounds for Section 301 action, with Korea and Brazil among the first targets in 1985 and the Korean investigation initiated on 4 November 1985, coinciding with the arrival of Japanese and newly industrialising country technology exports at competitive scale. Neither the historical nor the modern case has been analysed as a displacement-triggered sequence. The overclaiming risk lies in confounders: Cold War alignment delayed pressure on Japan and Korea, and TRIPS emerged from Uruguay Round dynamics with independent momentum.

## The Strongest Counterargument
The most damaging objection is that the sequence is real but uninformative, because displacement and escalation share a common cause and the ordering follows mechanically from it. Acemoglu, Aghion and Zilibotti (2006) already predict endogenous deceleration as distance to frontier closes; approaching the frontier is simultaneously what makes displacement measurable and what makes an incumbent notice. On that reading, escalation is epiphenomenal to a convergence ceiling that would bind regardless, and the observed order reflects only that measurement precedes reaction, which is true of any stimulus and any response. A second, compounding objection concerns independence: the four cases are causally linked, since German experience informed Japanese strategy, Japanese informed Korean, and all three inform Chinese policy, so a recurrent pattern may express one diffusing model of state-led catch-up rather than a regularity in incumbent behaviour. A third holds that Bown and Crowley (2013a) already establish the displacement-to-protection link with cleaner identification at the product level, leaving aggregation as the only increment.

These objections bound the claim rather than defeat it, provided the claim is stated as sequencing and not as causation. Endogenous deceleration and exogenous intervention are complements, not rivals, and the convergence literature's own decomposition cannot separate them, which is itself the finding. Case non-independence strengthens rather than weakens a pattern claim: if catch-up strategy diffuses while incumbent escalation nonetheless recurs across four structurally different incumbents and three centuries, the regularity attaches to the incumbent side, where no diffusion story is on offer. The increment over Bown and Crowley is specific and defensible, namely the systemic rather than product-bilateral level, the rhetorical stage that trade remedies data cannot observe, and a window long enough to include regime change in the instruments themselves. *The thesis fails if, in any of the four cases, documented rhetorical escalation or binding intervention can be dated systematically prior to the first measurable displacement in the affected technology class, or if the interval between displacement and policy is fully accounted for by administrative processing lags of the kind Destler (1992) describes.*

## Conclusion
The five literatures form a ring around the claim without closing it. Catch-up theory supplies the challenger's mechanism and freezes the incumbent; convergence research supplies the ceiling and attributes it entirely to domestic causes; protection models supply the equilibrium and omit the clock; technology denial supplies the instruments and times them to threat perception; intellectual property history supplies the imitation-then-enforcement pattern and never dates the transition. A study that endogenises incumbent response and tests its ordering against displacement metrics across Germany, Japan, South Korea and China therefore occupies a genuine and defensible position, on the condition that it claims sequence rather than cause, presents the 60-75 % frontier band as an observed regularity rather than an established threshold, bounds the novelty of contemporary upstream controls to their breadth and civilian orientation, and states plainly what evidence would overturn it.
